TRACK 5: SPONSY BOT - SPONSORSHIP OUTREACH AUTOMATION

OBJECTIVE

Build a human-in-the-loop sponsorship outreach system that helps a club find, prioritize, contact, and follow up with potential sponsors.

The system should:

- Research companies.
- Prioritize and categorize potential sponsors.
- Decide what to ask each sponsor for.
- Generate personalized sponsorship emails.
- Track every conversation and follow-up.
- Require organizer approval before any outgoing action.
- Replace scattered spreadsheets, forgotten follow-ups, and generic mass emails.

The final system should be practical enough for a club to realistically adopt for future sponsorship drives.


MANDATORY REQUIREMENTS

1. SPONSOR TRACKER

Maintain a sponsor tracker that records:

- Company name
- Company category or tier
- Contact person
- Contact information
- Current status
- Assigned team member
- Research information
- Sponsorship recommendation
- Reasoning
- Complete email history
- Follow-up history
- Notes

Required sponsor statuses:

- Not Contacted
- Contacted
- Follow-up
- Interested
- Declined
- Closed


2. COMPANY RESEARCH

Research every potential sponsor and use verified information to understand the company.

Research should include:

- Company profile
- Industry
- Products or services
- Previous sponsorships
- Past events supported
- Relevant partnerships
- Community or student initiatives
- Possible sponsorship opportunities

The system must use company research and past sponsorship history to prioritize and categorize sponsors.

IMPORTANT:

The system must never fabricate company information.

If information cannot be verified, clearly mark it as:

UNVERIFIED

Unverified information must never be presented as confirmed fact.


3. SPONSOR PRIORITIZATION AND RECOMMENDATION

The system should prioritize sponsors based on their profile and sponsorship history.

For every sponsor, recommend what the club should ask for.

Possible sponsorship requests include:

- Cash sponsorship
- Swag / Goodies
- Infrastructure
- Cloud credits
- Software credits
- Hardware
- Food / Refreshments
- Marketing support
- Other relevant benefits

The system must show the reasoning behind each recommendation.

Example:

Company: Cloud Provider

Recommendation: Cloud Infrastructure Credits

Reason:
The company provides cloud services and has previously supported developer or student communities.


4. PERSONALIZED EMAIL GENERATION

Generate a company-specific personalized sponsorship email for every sponsor.

The system must NOT send the same generic email to every company.

Each email should be based on:

- Company profile
- Company industry
- Previous sponsorship history
- Relevant initiatives
- Recommended sponsorship type
- Event information
- Contact person's role
- Other verified research information

The email should be personalized and relevant to the specific company.


5. HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP APPROVAL

This is a mandatory requirement.

NO EMAIL MAY BE SENT WITHOUT ORGANIZER APPROVAL.

The workflow must be:

Draft Email
    ↓
Organizer Reviews
    ↓
Organizer Edits if Necessary
    ↓
Organizer Approves
    ↓
Email Sent

The organizer must be able to:

- View the email draft
- Edit the subject
- Edit the email body
- Approve the email
- Reject the email
- Regenerate the email if necessary

The system must never bypass the approval step.


6. EMAIL SENDING

Send emails only after organizer approval.

For every sent email, track:

- Recipient
- Sender
- Subject
- Email body
- Sent time
- Approval status
- Sponsor
- Team member responsible for the outreach


7. REPLY TRACKING

Track sponsor replies.

Classify replies into:

- Interested
- No Reply
- Declined

The system should also provide a short summary of the reply and suggest the next action where appropriate.


8. FOLLOW-UP AUTOMATION

Schedule follow-ups automatically while respecting a fixed limit.

Example:

Initial Email
    ↓
Follow-up 1
    ↓
Follow-up 2
    ↓
STOP

Maximum:

2 follow-ups per sponsor.

The system must:

- Never send unlimited follow-ups.
- Never spam a sponsor.
- Stop follow-ups after the maximum limit.
- Stop follow-ups if the sponsor declines.
- Stop follow-ups if the sponsor becomes interested.
- Respect the human approval workflow for outgoing follow-ups.


9. INTERESTED SPONSORS

When a sponsor becomes interested:

- Move the sponsor to the Interested stage.
- Stop unnecessary follow-ups.
- Move the sponsor to the next step.
- Provide a way to arrange a meeting.

Possible workflow:

Interested
    ↓
Meeting Requested
    ↓
Meeting Scheduled
    ↓
Negotiation
    ↓
Won / Closed


10. DUPLICATE OUTREACH PREVENTION

Prevent duplicate outreach.

Two team members must never unknowingly contact the same company.

Before sending an email, the system must check:

- Whether the company already exists.
- Whether the company has already been contacted.
- Which team member is handling the company.
- Previous email history.

The system should clearly show who owns or is responsible for each sponsor.


11. FREE APIs AND FREE TIERS

Use only free APIs or free-tier APIs.

The system must use API limits intelligently.

The expected volume is approximately:

20-40 outgoing emails per day.

The system should be designed to operate within free-tier API limits and must not bypass rate limits.


12. DATA ACCURACY

The system must never fabricate:

- Company details
- Sponsorship history
- Contact information
- Previous partnerships
- Other company information

Only verified information should be presented as fact.

Unverified information must be clearly marked as:

UNVERIFIED

GOOD-TO-HAVE FEATURES

1. AUTOMATIC CONTACT DISCOVERY

Automatically find relevant contacts at each company where possible.

Examples:

- Sponsorship Manager
- Marketing Manager
- HR
- Business Development
- CSR
- Community Manager
- Relevant decision maker


2. ONE-CLICK AUTOMATION

Provide a one-click workflow where organizer approval triggers the appropriate next step.

For example:

Approve
    ↓
Send Email
    ↓
Track Email
    ↓
Schedule Follow-up
    ↓
Track Reply
    ↓
Move to Next Stage

However, the system must still respect the human approval requirement for outgoing emails.


3. DASHBOARD

Create a dashboard showing:

- Total sponsors
- Sponsors by pipeline stage
- Not contacted
- Contacted
- Follow-up pending
- Interested
- Declined
- Meetings
- Sponsorships won
- Response rate
- Follow-up rate
- Money secured
- Benefits / credits secured


4. MEETING BOOKING

Provide meeting booking functionality using:

- Calendar link
- Google Calendar
- Other suitable free scheduling solution


5. SPONSORSHIP BROCHURE / DECK

Allow a sponsorship brochure or deck to be attached to emails.

The attachment can be tailored according to the sponsor tier.


6. REUSABLE EMAIL TEMPLATES

Provide reusable templates for:

- First outreach
- Follow-up 1
- Follow-up 2
- Interested response
- Meeting request
- Thank-you email


7. GOOGLE SHEETS INTEGRATION

Provide Google Sheets integration for importing or exporting sponsor tracker information.

The main application database should remain the source of truth.


8. REPLY SUMMARIES AND SUGGESTED RESPONSES

When a sponsor replies:

- Summarize the reply.
- Identify the sponsor's intent.
- Suggest an appropriate response.
- Allow the organizer to review and edit the response.


9. NOTIFICATIONS

Notify organizers when:

- A sponsor replies.
- A sponsor becomes interested.
- A follow-up is due.
- A meeting is requested.
- A sponsorship is successfully secured.


10. INSTITUTIONAL MEMORY

Store useful information about previous sponsors for future sponsorship drives.

Examples:

- Previous sponsors
- What they sponsored
- Amount or benefits provided
- Previous communication
- Previous event
- Sponsor preferences
- Outcome of previous collaboration

This information should help future teams make better sponsorship decisions.


EXAMPLE

Suppose the club needs sponsors for a 24-hour hackathon.

Requirement:

- Cash sponsorship: INR 1,50,000+
- Swag / Goodies
- Infrastructure / Cloud Credits

The club has a list of 60 companies.

The system should:

1. Research the 60 companies.
2. Verify available information.
3. Identify relevant contacts.
4. Analyze previous sponsorship history.
5. Prioritize the companies.
6. Categorize them into suitable sponsorship types.
7. Recommend what to ask each company for.
8. Explain the reasoning.
9. Generate personalized emails.
10. Put emails into the organizer approval queue.
11. Allow the organizer to edit the emails.
12. Send only approved emails.
13. Track replies.
14. Classify replies as Interested, No Reply, or Declined.
15. Follow up with companies that do not reply.
16. Allow a maximum of two follow-ups.
17. Stop contacting companies that decline.
18. Move interested companies toward a meeting.
19. Prevent duplicate outreach.
20. Track the complete sponsorship pipeline.


WHAT NOT TO DO

DO NOT:

- Build a generic mass-mailer.
- Send the same email to every company.
- Send emails automatically without human review and approval.
- Spam sponsors with unlimited follow-ups.
- Use paid APIs when free alternatives are available.
- Ignore free-tier API rate limits.
- Fabricate company details.
- Fabricate sponsorship history.
- Fabricate contact information.
- Build only a simple ChatGPT/Gemini wrapper that generates emails while tracking remains manual.
- Lose track of which team member contacted which company.
- Allow multiple team members to unknowingly contact the same sponsor.
- Focus only on UI polish while the actual outreach workflow remains manual.
- Leave the core sponsorship workflow unimplemented.

4. Quality of sponsor categorization
5. Reliability of tracking
6. Reliability of follow-ups

9. Technical implementation
10. Usability
